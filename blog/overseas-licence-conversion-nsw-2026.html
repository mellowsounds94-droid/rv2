Converting Your Overseas Licence to a NSW Licence: What Changed in 2026

If you've recently moved to Western Sydney with an overseas driving licence, the rules around converting it to a NSW licence changed significantly on 1 February 2026 — and the change affects more drivers than the previous rules did. Here's what actually applies to you, and what to expect from the process.

Do You Need to Convert at All?

If you're moving to NSW and plan to keep driving, yes — and there's a deadline. Permanent residents need to obtain a NSW driver licence within 3 months of living in NSW to continue driving. Temporary visitors need to obtain at least a temporary NSW driver licence within 6 months. Driving on an expired visitor status or beyond these windows on your original overseas licence isn't just a technicality — it can mean you're driving unlicensed in NSW.

List A vs List B: Which Country Are You From?

NSW splits overseas licences into two groups, and which one your country falls into determines what you'll need to do:

List A (“Recognised Countries”) — if you hold a valid car licence from a List A country (this includes the UK, USA, New Zealand, and Canada, among others), you generally don't need to sit a knowledge test or driving test to convert, provided your licence is current or expired within the last 5 years. You'll still need to visit a service centre, prove your identity, and go through the standard conversion paperwork — but you skip the testing requirement.

List B — if your licence is from a List B country, the rules are now stricter than they used to be. You'll need to check your specific country's status, since which list applies can vary and has recently been reviewed by the NSW Government.

What Changed on 1 February 2026

Before this date, drivers aged 25 and over from many List B countries could convert their overseas licence without sitting a knowledge test or driving test, under long-standing “experienced driver” exemptions. From 1 February 2026, those exemptions have been removed for a broader group of List B countries. Drivers 25 and over from these countries must now pass both the Driver Knowledge Test and the Driving Test before their NSW licence is issued — the same requirement that already applied to List B drivers under 25.

The NSW Government's stated reasoning is road safety consistency: bringing overseas licence conversion in line with the same testing standards that already apply to younger drivers, rather than allowing older, more experienced drivers to skip testing based on age alone.

To manage the expected increase in test bookings, Service NSW has temporarily added extra driving test capacity — including additional Saturday testing slots at metropolitan service centres — through February 2026. If you're affected by this change, it's worth booking early and checking availability regularly, since demand for test slots is likely to be higher than usual during this transition period.

What the Testing Actually Involves

If you fall under the List B testing requirement, you'll need to pass:

  • The Driver Knowledge Test — a computer-based test on NSW road rules and signage. Even experienced overseas drivers often find this test covers rules, penalties, and give-way situations that differ from what they're used to.
  • The Driving Test — the same practical test NSW learners sit, assessing your ability to drive safely and confidently under NSW conditions, including local road rules, giving way, and parking manoeuvres.

Unlike a NSW learner starting from scratch, you won't need to complete a learner logbook if you've previously held an overseas licence — but you will need to be genuinely comfortable with NSW-specific road rules and driving conventions before sitting the tests, since these can differ meaningfully from what you're used to, even if you've been driving safely for years elsewhere.

Getting Ready for the Knowledge and Driving Tests

Because this affects experienced drivers who may not have needed formal lessons in years, preparation looks a little different than it does for a first-time learner. It's less about learning to drive from zero, and more about:

  • Getting familiar with NSW-specific road rules, signage, and give-way conventions
  • Practising in local traffic conditions, including areas around your nearest test centre
  • Building confidence with any conventions that differ from your home country (roundabout rules, school zone timing, and mobile phone laws are common adjustment points)

Quick Answers

Do I need to sit a driving test to convert my overseas licence to a NSW licence?

It depends on your country. List A countries like the UK, USA, New Zealand, and Canada generally don't require testing if your licence is current or expired within 5 years. List B countries now require both a Knowledge Test and Driving Test for drivers of all ages, following the 1 February 2026 rule change.

How long do I have to convert my licence after moving to NSW?

Permanent residents must convert within 3 months of moving to NSW. Temporary visitors need at least a temporary NSW licence within 6 months.

This article reflects the NSW Government's overseas licence conversion reforms effective 1 February 2026, as published by Service NSW and Transport for NSW. Country list classifications and testing requirements can change — always check your specific country's current status on the Transport for NSW knowledge and driving test exemptions page before relying on this guide.
