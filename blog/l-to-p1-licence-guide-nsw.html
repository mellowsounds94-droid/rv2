L to P1 in NSW: A Complete Guide to Getting Your Provisional Licence

Getting from your learner licence to your red P plates in NSW involves a few clear steps — a logbook, two tests, and a set of rules that apply once you're driving solo. None of it is complicated on its own, but it helps to know what's coming before you're in the middle of it. Here's the full path from L to P1, in order.

Step 1: Hold Your Learner Licence and Start Logging Hours

Once you've got your learner licence, the clock starts on two requirements if you're under 25: you need to hold your learner licence for at least 12 months, and you need to log 120 hours of supervised driving, including at least 20 hours of night driving (between sunset and sunrise). If you're 25 or over, neither of these applies — you can attempt the Hazard Perception Test and Driving Test as soon as you feel ready, with no logbook required.

Hours can be logged either in the paper logbook issued with your learner licence, or through the official Roundtrip digital logbook app, which Transport for NSW now uses in place of the older Licence Ready and L2P apps.

Step 2: Log Your 120 Hours (and Use the 3-for-1 Rule)

This is where a lot of learners underestimate how quickly the hours can add up. Under Transport for NSW's “3 for 1” bonus hours scheme, every hour of a structured lesson with a licensed NSW driving instructor can be logged as three hours of supervised driving — so a 1-hour lesson counts as 3 logbook hours. The bonus is capped at 10 instructor hours, meaning a maximum of 20 bonus hours can be logged this way, but it's still a meaningful head start.

If you're under 25 and have completed at least 50 hours of supervised driving, you're also eligible for the NSW Safer Drivers Course. Completing it adds a further 20 hours of logbook credit, on top of genuinely useful training in hazard awareness, speed management, and safe following distances — skills that don't always come up naturally in day-to-day supervised practice.

A quick note on honesty here: false entries in a logbook carry real penalties, including fines and being barred from attempting the Driving Test for up to six weeks. It's not worth cutting corners — the hours exist to make sure you're genuinely road-ready.

Step 3: Pass the Hazard Perception Test (HPT)

Before you can book the Driving Test, you need to pass the Hazard Perception Test — a computer-based test of 15 short videos where you need to score 55 or higher out of 75. If you're under 25, you can book the HPT once you've held your learner licence for at least 10 months (so you can sit it slightly before you hit your full 12 months and 120 hours, and be ready to book the Driving Test as soon as you qualify). If you're 25 or over, you can book it whenever you're ready.

You can resit the HPT as many times as needed, but you'll pay the test fee each time — so it's worth genuinely preparing rather than treating the first attempt as a practice run.

Step 4: Pass the Driving Test and Get Your Red Ps

Once you've passed the HPT and met your logbook and time requirements, you can book the Driving Test. On the day, you'll need to bring your completed logbook (or have your digital hours submitted), prove your identity, and pass an eyesight test. If you fail, you'll need to wait at least seven days before trying again — so it's worth being genuinely test-ready rather than testing early just to “see how it goes.”

Passing the Driving Test gets you your P1 licence — the red Ps — which is the first stage that allows you to drive without a supervisor.

Life on Your P1: What Changes

Red Ps come with real conditions worth knowing before your first solo drive:

  • A speed limit of 90 km/h, regardless of the posted limit
  • Zero alcohol
  • No supervising learner drivers
  • Mandatory P plates displayed front and back
  • No mobile phone use at all while driving, including hands-free
  • If you're under 25, a “peer passenger” restriction — no more than one passenger under 21 between 11pm and 5am, unless you hold an exemption
  • A demerit point limit of just 4 points in a 3-year period before your licence is suspended

You'll hold your P1 for a minimum of 12 months before you can apply for your P2 (green Ps) — and your P1 is only valid for 18 months, so it's worth applying for your green Ps within that window. From P2, you'll need to hold that licence for at least 24 months before you're eligible for your full, unrestricted licence.

Quick Answers

How long do I need to hold my P1 licence before upgrading to P2?

At least 12 months, and your P1 is only valid for 18 months, so you'll want to apply for your P2 within that window.

What's the speed limit on P1 (red P) plates in NSW?

90 km/h, regardless of the posted speed limit, along with zero alcohol and a 4-demerit-point limit over 3 years.

This article reflects Transport for NSW requirements as published at nsw.gov.au, including logbook, Hazard Perception Test, and provisional licence rules current as of 2026.
